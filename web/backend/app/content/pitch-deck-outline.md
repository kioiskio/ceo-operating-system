# Pitch Deck Outline

> A slide-by-slide structure tested across 500+ funded startup decks (Sequoia, YC, a16z derivation).
> Customize but don't skip slides. Investors expect this narrative arc.

---

## Slide 1: Title / One-Liner

**Goal:** Investor knows what you do in 5 seconds.

**Content:**
- Company name and logo
- One sentence value proposition (no jargon)
- Optional: tagline that hints at the category

**Example:** "Acme — CRM for construction subcontractors. Close 2× more bids without a sales team."

---

## Slide 2: Problem

**Goal:** Investor feels the pain personally.

**Content:**
- The specific problem your customer has today
- Quantify it: dollars lost, hours wasted, customers unhappy
- Current alternatives and why they suck

**Example:**
> Subcontractors spend 12 hours/week chasing bid updates across phone calls, texts, and emails.
> 34% of bids are lost because follow-ups fall through the cracks.
> Existing CRMs are built for enterprise sales teams — unusable on a job site.

---

## Slide 3: Solution

**Goal:** Investor sees how your product changes the customer's world.

**Content:**
- Product screenshot, demo GIF, or mockup (visual, not text-heavy)
- Before/after: what the customer's life looks like with your product
- Why you're defensible (network effects, data moat, switching costs, IP)

---

## Slide 4: Market Size

**Goal:** Investor sees a big enough prize.

**Content:**
- **Bottom-up TAM:** # of target customers × annual contract value
- **SOM** (Serviceable Obtainable Market): the realistic slice you can capture in 3-5 years
- Market growth rate and tailwinds
- "Why now?" — the tectonic shift that makes this possible

**Avoid:** "Gartner says the market is $500B." Investors tune out.

---

## Slide 5: Traction

**Goal:** Investor sees momentum and de-risks the bet.

**Content (choose what applies):**
- Revenue (MRR/ARR, growth rate, ideally with a chart)
- User growth (with retention — not just top-line downloads)
- Key customer logos or case studies
- Unit economics: CAC, LTV, payback period
- If pre-revenue: LOIs, pilot results, waitlist size, design partner commitments

---

## Slide 6: Business Model

**Goal:** Investor understands the engine.

**Content:**
- Revenue model: subscription, transaction, marketplace, usage-based?
- Specific pricing (not "freemium" — actual price points)
- Unit economics per customer
- Path to profitability (when do you cross over?)

---

## Slide 7: Competition

**Goal:** Investor trusts you understand the landscape.

**Content:**
- Honest competitive matrix (2×2 or feature comparison table)
- Your unique advantage (not "we work harder")
- Moat: why can't incumbents copy this in a quarter?

**Avoid:** "We have no competitors." There is always a competitor — even if it's "manual process" or "Excel."

---

## Slide 8: Go-to-Market

**Goal:** Investor sees a repeatable customer acquisition engine.

**Content:**
- Specific channels (not "content marketing and word of mouth")
- CAC by channel with actual data or well-researched assumptions
- Sales motion: self-serve, product-led, inside sales, or enterprise?
- 12-month GTM timeline with milestones

---

## Slide 9: Team

**Goal:** Investor believes *this* team can win.

**Content:**
- Founders: relevant background, why you're uniquely qualified
- Key hires already made
- Critical hires needed in next 6 months
- Advisors or angels that lend credibility

**Format:** Headshots + 3 bullet points per person. Not paragraphs.

---

## Slide 10: Financials & Ask

**Goal:** Investor knows exactly what you need and why.

**Content:**
- Ask amount (specific number)
- Use of funds breakdown (engineering: X%, GTM: Y%, ops: Z%)
- Projected runway and key milestones this money buys
- What metrics will justify the next round?

---

## Appendix Slides (not in main deck, but in data room)

- Detailed financial model (3-year projections)
- Customer case studies / testimonials
- Product roadmap (next 12 months)
- Competitive deep-dive
- Current cap table
- Press coverage
