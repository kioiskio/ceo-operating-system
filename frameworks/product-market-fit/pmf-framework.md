# Product-Market Fit Framework

> **Stage:** Pre-seed through Series A
> **Last updated:** May 2026

A structured approach to measuring and systematically achieving product-market fit — not as a binary milestone, but as a spectrum you continuously improve.

---

## The PMF Spectrum

PMF is not a switch that flips. It's a gradient:

```
No PMF ──────── Weak PMF ──────── Strong PMF ──────── Extreme PMF
 "No one cares"   "Some users     "Customers         "Customers
                  stick around"    would be upset     are pulling
                                   if you died"      the product
                                                      out of you"
```

## How to Measure PMF

### Leading Indicators (early-stage)

| Metric | Weak PMF | Strong PMF | How to Measure |
|--------|----------|------------|----------------|
| Retention (D30) | <20% | >40% (consumer) / >70% (B2B SaaS) | Cohort analysis |
| Organic growth | <10% of new users | >30% of new users | Attribution tracking |
| Time to value | >7 days | <1 day | User analytics |
| Customer calls | "It's interesting" | "When can I pay you?" | Direct interviews |
| NPS | <20 | >40 | In-app survey |

### Lagging Indicators (growth-stage)

| Metric | Weak PMF | Strong PMF |
|--------|----------|------------|
| Net Revenue Retention | <100% | >120% |
| Logo churn (monthly) | >5% | <2% |
| Expansion revenue | <10% | >30% of new ARR |
| Sales cycle | >90 days | <30 days |
| Win rate | <20% | >40% |

## The PMF Engine: 4 Levers

### Lever 1: Problem Validation
The problem must be:
- **Frequent:** Customer encounters it regularly (daily/weekly, not annually)
- **Painful:** They've tried to solve it before (and failed)
- **Urgent:** There's a deadline or cost to inaction
- **Recognized:** Customer identifies it themselves (you're not educating them that they have a problem)

**Test:** Ask 10 target customers "How are you solving this today?" If they all say "we're not," you don't have a problem worth solving.

### Lever 2: Solution Fit
Your solution must deliver a **before/after transformation** that the customer can describe in their own words.

**Test:** "If we shut down tomorrow, what would you lose?" Answers that describe process/workflow loss (not just inconvenience) signal PMF.

### Lever 3: Target Segment Precision

Broad PMF is a myth. PMF happens for **specific segments**, not for "everyone."

**Segmentation dimensions:**
- Company size (1-10, 10-50, 50-200, 200+)
- Industry vertical
- Buyer persona (who signs the check vs who uses the product)
- Geography
- Tech maturity (cloud-native vs on-premise legacy)

**Action:** Identify your one best segment (highest retention + lowest CAC) and go all-in. Resist the urge to expand horizontally before you own one vertical.

### Lever 4: Value Delivery Speed

The single biggest PMF killer is slow time-to-value.

| Phase | Benchmark |
|-------|-----------|
| Sign-up to first "aha moment" | <5 minutes (consumer) / <1 day (B2B) |
| First value to habitual use | <7 days |
| Habitual use to evangelism | <30 days |

**Action:** Map your user's first 7 days. Find the longest gap between steps. Kill it.

## The PMF Interview (Sean Ellis Test)

Ask your existing users:

> "How would you feel if you could no longer use [product]?"

- **Very disappointed** → PMF signal
- **Somewhat disappointed** → Useful but replaceable
- **Not disappointed** → No PMF

**Benchmark:** If >40% say "very disappointed," you have PMF. If <25%, you don't.

## PMF Playbook by Stage

### Pre-Seed: Find the Problem
- Talk to 50 potential customers (not friends, not other founders)
- Do NOT build yet. Document the problem and existing workarounds.
- Identify 3-5 design partners willing to pay before you build.

### Seed: Find the Solution
- Ship the smallest thing that delivers the core transformation.
- Measure D7 and D30 retention obsessively.
- Kill features that don't improve retention.
- When 40%+ of users say "very disappointed" on the Sean Ellis test, you've crossed the threshold.

### Series A: Scale the Fit
- Double down on your best segment.
- Build for expansion revenue (upsells, add-ons, seat expansion).
- Document your ICP (Ideal Customer Profile) so precisely that sales can pre-qualify in 3 questions.

## Warning Signs You Don't Have PMF

1. **You're explaining what the product does** (instead of customers telling you)
2. **Growth requires increasing spend** (not organic or word-of-mouth)
3. **Churn is constant or increasing** (not improving month over month)
4. **Your roadmap is driven by prospects, not power users**
5. **You dread customer interviews** (because the feedback is consistently lukewarm)
6. **"We just need more features"** — More features don't create PMF; they disguise the lack of it.
